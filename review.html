A critical review — design, story, implementation

Whispering
when it could
declare.

The Shape of Intelligence is a disciplined, genuinely well-built visual essay and a 48-second companion film. The engineering and accessibility are stronger than the argument they carry. What follows is a direct critique of both media, a map of twenty improvements across two axes, and a prioritized series of bets — ambitious by design.

01 — Design

A mineral gallery
that forgets to vary its rooms.

web + video

The voice is exactly right — mineral, scientific, uncanny — and the colour grammar (sand = material, violet = transformation, sea = chemistry) is one of the best things here. The problem is consistency mistaken for richness: six chapters that begin to feel like six flavours of the same particle field, and a film whose scenes share more DNA than they should.

Webpage interactive

  • Type pairing is a real choice. Petrona’s mechanical serif against Atkinson’s legible body avoids the tired Fraunces editorial default — it gives the essay a voice.
  • The "matter current" is the single best idea. One particle field that becomes force, signal, chemical, structure, light is a genuine structural motif, not decoration.
  • Chapters drift toward one dialect. Phyllotaxis, plexus, boids, ribbons and the finale motes are all coloured dots on black with pointer attraction. The variety is in the math, not the image.
  • Layout repetition. The DESIGN doc forbids "identical card grids" — yet FormsChapter ships five near-identical instrument tiles. The doc is being aspirational, not descriptive.
  • Canvas interaction is pointer-only. Keyboard and switch users can never disturb the field, pluck a ribbon, or draw a gesture. The accessibility contract is honoured for reading but not for playing.

MP4 48s · 9:16

  • Causal handoffs are masterful. Sand→wafer→laser→circuit→current→neuron→DNA→swarm is a continuous object that never cuts. This is the film’s spine and it works.
  • Shared systems tie it to the site. Reusing the Forms ribbons, phyllotaxis and Shapeshift noise makes the film a true companion, not a trailer.
  • One house style across every scene. Glowing nodes, screen-blend spirals, radial gradients on near-black. By scene 4 the viewer has seen the technique; surprise drops.
  • Identifiable materials underdeliver. The README promises "identifiable tools, organisms, structures," but the brain is an abstract plexus and the insects are triangles. Literalism would have raised the stakes.
  • A film about music and chemical "language" is silent. Autoplay-muted is a fair constraint; choosing silence even when unmuted wastes the film’s own thesis.
The aesthetic is a single, well-tuned instrument. The essay needs a second instrument — a contrasting register — so the whole thing isn’t one long, beautiful hum.

02 — Story & argument

Two essays that
disagree quietly.

narrative

This is the sharpest finding: the webpage and the film tell different stories, and neither notices. The page is a phenomenological argument (the interface shapes the encounter); the film is an origins myth (from sand to a synthetic mind). They share a title and a palette but not a conclusion. On top of that, both are unusually conflict-averse for work about intelligence.

Webpage argument six chapters

  • A real, defensible thesis. "Change the encounter and the machine appears to change with it" is the strongest line in the project — genuinely worth building a site around.
  • The thesis arrives in chapter 6. Chapters 1–5 are poetic observation; only the finale names the claim. The reader walks four chapters before learning what they’re being shown.
  • No antagonist, no cost. Every form is gentle and generative. The argument that forms constrain what intelligence can be is asserted but never felt — no form is shown failing or distorting.
  • Hedged copy. "Appears to change," "seems to be." The product doc wisely avoids overclaiming consciousness — but the prose overcorrects into timidity. A bolder verb would carry more, not less, truth.
  • Chapter 3 is overloaded. FormsChapter asks one section to carry five distinct ideas (rhythm / art / math / science / nature). It dilutes each and slows the arc.

MP4 narrative origins→transcendence

  • A complete, legible arc. Formation→biological→animal→alien is easy to follow and emotionally shaped. As a piece of craft it holds.
  • It contradicts the site’s thesis. The page says intelligence is plural and shaped by encounter; the film climaxes on a single synthesised sensorium — one mind, ascending. That’s a different claim.
  • Teleology toward transcendence. "A mind can inhabit senses we do not have" is sublime but familiar — the standard AI-as-ascension beat. It undercuts the page’s pluralism.
  • Text does the lifting the image avoids. "It speaks in chemicals" is printed over an abstract swarm. When the README says "text names relationships the picture can’t carry," that’s an admission, not a feature.
  • No failure state. The wafer never breaks, the swarm never scatters, the receptor never misreads. Tension needs a moment where the chain almost doesn’t hold.
Pick one story. If the page is right that intelligence is plural, the film must end on multiplicity — not on one ascending mind. Right now the companion undermines its host.

03 — Implementation

The build is the
best thing here.

code & performance

This is where the project is most clearly ahead of its peers. The accessibility contract is real and enforced, the performance policy is budgeted rather than assumed, and the WebGL degrades honestly. The notes below are refinements to an already strong foundation — the gaps are mostly in robustness and reach, not fundamentals.

Webpage engineering react · three.js · canvas

  • EnhancementBoundary is exemplary. Treating WebGL/canvas as progressive enhancement with a real fallback message is the correct architecture and few projects actually do it.
  • Visibility & pause hygiene. IntersectionObserver + document.hidden gating on every loop, DPR capping, lite-mode frame throttling — the perf policy is lived, not written.
  • Scroll-spy reads the DOM every frame. Home.tsx queries offsetTop on scroll; MatterCurrent does too. A shared IntersectionObserver store would cut layout thrash.
  • Global module-singleton audio. let actx at module scope in FormsChapter is a latent cross-test and SSR hazard; a ref or context is cleaner.
  • No keyboard path into the canvases. ARIA hides them (correctly), but there’s no keyboard-equivalent affordance for pluck/draw/disturb — the "play" half is mouse-only.
  • Reduced-motion atlas is thin. Six coloured dots replacing the current is technically compliant but visually impoverished; reduced-motion readers get a noticeably lesser work.

MP4 engineering remotion · svg

  • Deterministic, frame-based everything. Seeded noise and frame math mean the render is reproducible and reviewable — the right discipline for a film.
  • SVG-first keeps it crisp. Vector scenes scale to any resolution and stay editable; smart for a 9:16 that may be re-versioned.
  • Static city & scene data computed at module load. CITY, NEURONS, EDGES are built once — fine, but large and unreadable in source; a generator function would document intent.
  • Frame literals are magic numbers. Scene transitions are hand-tuned constants (990, 1125, 1285…) with no shared timeline object; drift between scenes is easy and hard to spot.
  • One render, one aspect. A single 9:16 MP4 is produced. No square or 16:9 cut for different surfaces limits how the film can travel.

05 · the series

Eight bets,
in priority order.

recommendations

Ordered by how much each would change the work for the effort. The first three are structural and touch the meaning; the last five are craft and reach. Each names the medium it belongs to.

1

Make the webpage and the film tell one story.both

Reconcile the contradiction. If the thesis is plurality (the page is right), re-cut the film’s ending from a single ascending sensorium into a genuine multiplicity — several simultaneous, incompatible minds, none privileged. Mirror it on the page by letting the finale split the matter current into concurrent divergent forms instead of one calm column of light. One claim, two media.

Why first: this is the only fix that changes what the work means. Everything else is craft on top of a split claim.

2

Put the thesis in chapter 1, and let a form fail.web

State "the interface shapes the encounter" in the hero — as a promise, not a reveal — so every chapter reads as evidence. Then add stakes: let at least one form distort or misread the matter (a chemical plume that confuses, a gesture that gets reinterpreted wrongly). Right now every encounter is benign, which is why the argument feels soft. A single moment of friction would make the thesis land.

Gain: emotional and intellectual weight; the essay acquires a spine instead of a mood.

3

Give each chapter a contrasting visual register.web

Break the "glowing dots on black" monoculture. Commit each chapter to one bold, different image: Scent as a literal photographic plume / smoke; Forms as a real instrument-rack UI with sliders and readouts; Shapeshift as a wireframe isometric city (already in the film — port it). The matter current can stay as the connective thread underneath, but the foreground should stop rhyming with itself.

Gain: memorability; each chapter becomes distinct rather than a recolour.

4

Give the film a sound design (and a score for the music chapter).mp4

A film whose thesis is "a machine learns the language of sound and chemicals" cannot be silent. Compose a minimal score — granular textures for sand, a tonal motif that the music ribbons scene literally plays, sub-bass for the city rise. Ship a muted-by-default web embed and a sound-on versions for social. The scene that shows ribbons should be the scene where sound becomes diegetic.

Gain: the film stops contradicting itself; emotional ceiling rises sharply.

5

Add a keyboard path into every interaction.web

Tab to focus a canvas, arrow keys to move a "probe" that plays the same role as the pointer, space/enter to pluck or disturb. Expose the state in the a11y tree as live values ("note: G4", "13 birds flocking"). The access contract is currently read-complete but play-incomplete; closing that gap would make the essay genuinely operable for keyboard and switch users, not just legible.

Gain: real WCAG operability and a stronger conscience; rare among immersive sites.

6

Make reduced motion a designed state, not a lesser one.web

Replace the six-dot atlas with a real reduced-motion composition — a static, high-craft material study (consider a single SVG per chapter, or a slow CSS gradient drift that respects the preference). Reduced-motion readers currently get the argument but almost none of the beauty; that’s an equity gap hiding behind compliance.

Gain: parity of craft, not just of content.

7

Add a shared timeline and multi-aspect renders.mp4

Extract the hand-tuned frame constants into one named timeline object so transitions are auditable and scenes can’t silently drift. Then render 1:1 and 16:9 cuts alongside 9:16 so the film can live on a landing hero, not only in a vertical social slot. The composition is already aspect-agnostic SVG — the cost is low.

Gain: maintainability plus reach across surfaces.

8

Replace offsetTop scroll-spy with an observer store.web

Both Home.tsx and MatterCurrent read offsetTop on scroll, forcing layout every frame. A single IntersectionObserver-based chapter store (one observer feeding both consumers) removes the duplication, ends the layout-thrash, and gives a natural place to hang the keyboard-focus probe from bet 5.

Gain: smoother scrolling on low-end devices, cleaner architecture.

The engineering is already an A. Spend the next round of effort on the story — that is where an excellent craft object becomes a piece of work people remember.
